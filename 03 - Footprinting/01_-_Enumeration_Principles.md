# Enumeration Principles

> **Core idea:** Enumeration is not just about what you can *see* — it's equally about reasoning over what you *can't* see, and why. Experience teaches you to notice the components that aren't obvious at first glance.

## The Guiding Questions

Whenever you approach any target, run through this mental checklist. It works in essentially any situation because it forces you to think beyond the surface-level results of a scan.

**What we can see:**
- What can we see?
- What reasons can we have for seeing it?
- What image does what we see create for us?
- What do we gain from it?
- How can we use it?

**What we cannot see:**
- What can we *not* see?
- What reasons can there be that we do not see it?
- What image results from what we do not see?

> **Why this matters:** A closed or filtered port, a missing service banner, or a suppressed version string is *information*. The absence of something often tells you about firewalls, hardening, or segmentation just as clearly as an open port tells you about an exposed service.

## The Three Principles

| No. | Principle |
| --- | --------- |
| 1 | There is more than meets the eye. Consider all points of view. |
| 2 | Distinguish between what we see and what we do not see. |
| 3 | There are always ways to gain more information. Understand the target. |

## Takeaway

Treat every scan result as a starting point for a question, not an end in itself. The goal of enumeration is to build an accurate mental model of the target — its services, its purpose, its trust relationships, and its weak points — before you ever attempt to interact aggressively with it.
